December 13, 2022 · Courtry, Paris region, France

Bruno Llambrich: Le Prédateur des Bois

Content note This case involves serial rape and a suicide in pretrial detention. Victims are not named or shown. A real private individual, a distant cousin, is discussed without being named beyond what is already public record. The legal question is still unsettled, so this page keeps to the public record.

A man committed five rapes in the Paris region and was never matched to them for over a decade. A mail-order DNA kit, the FBI and a banned technique finally put a name to the profile. The hosts agree on who did it. They split on whether the way he was found should ever be allowed again.

1998 to 2008

Five attacks, one profile

Bruno Llambrich committed five documented rapes in the Paris region between 1998 and 2008. DNA was recovered at every scene, but it never matched anyone in France’s national database.

  • The crimesFive documented rapes, Paris region, 1998 to 2008
  • The evidenceThe same DNA profile, five times
  • The databaseNo match in FNAEG, the national DNA database
  • The appealInvestigators circulated sketches in a public call for witnesses
  1. 01 / 04 · THE BAN

    Recreational and ancestry-focused genetic testing is criminally prohibited for French citizens under Article 16-10 of the Civil Code, with a fine of up to €3,750.

  2. 02 / 04 · THE WORKAROUND

    In August 2021, investigating judges used a rogatory request to have the FBI run the crime-scene profile against a US commercial genealogy database. Reporting calls it the first confirmed use of the technique in French criminal history.

  3. 03 / 04 · THE COUSIN

    The search surfaced a distant cousin who had submitted his own DNA to a US ancestry service for personal reasons. He had no involvement in the crimes. Conventional genealogical research then narrowed the tree to Llambrich.

  4. 04 / 04 · THE ARREST

    Llambrich was arrested at his home in Courtry on December 13, 2022. Paris chief prosecutor Laure Beccuau confirmed the FBI’s involvement and the method.

The tool or the route

Same case
Eli’s reading

Fix the law, keep the tool.

The cousin’s privacy was violated, and that is not in dispute. A narrower version, with a judge’s sign-off before a request goes out, hard retention limits and notification, would answer what happened to him without giving up a tool that caught a real predator.

Simone’s reading

The ends don’t justify how the state got there.

A badly worded law is an accident you fix with better wording. A workaround chosen with open eyes, around an explicit prohibition, is not a drafting problem. And CNIL’s history of scope creep says safeguards on paper tend to erode.

No court ever ruled on the 2021 to 2022 use of the technique, because Llambrich died before trial. The claim that investigators knew the legal risk going in comes from a single Radio France feature.
