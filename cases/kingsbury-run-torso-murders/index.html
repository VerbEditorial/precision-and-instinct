September 1934 to August 1938 · Cleveland, Ohio

The Mad Butcher of Kingsbury Run

Content note This case involves a serial killer who dismembered his victims. No victim is shown and graphic detail is kept out of this page. Most of the victims were never identified. All the named suspects and investigators are long dead, and the case is officially unsolved.

Between 1934 and 1938 someone left at least twelve bodies in and around Cleveland’s Kingsbury Run. Only two were ever named, the police records have since vanished, and the man sent to stop it, Eliot Ness, never did. The hosts agree on how little is known. They split on whether a good read is worth a verdict.

1934 to 1938

The ravine and the lakefront

The killer’s victims were found in and around Kingsbury Run, Cleveland, and along the Lake Erie shore. Most were decapitated and many were dismembered. The police never made a case against anyone.

  • The victimsAt least 12 official, some sources count 13
  • The placeKingsbury Run and the Lake Erie shore, Cleveland
  • The namesOnly two ever identified, both by fingerprints
  • The recordsAll official police records lost, destroyed or removed
  1. 01 / 04 · THE COUNT

    At least 12 official victims, found between September 1934 and August 1938. Some sources count 13, including the 1934 “Lady of the Lake”.

  2. 02 / 04 · THE NAMES

    Only two were ever positively identified, Edward Andrassy and Florence Polillo, both by fingerprints. A third, tentatively Rose Wallace, was never officially confirmed.

  3. 03 / 04 · THE RECORDS

    According to the Cleveland Police Museum, all official police records on the case have since been lost, destroyed or removed.

  4. 04 / 04 · THE NEW WORK

    In August 2024 the Cuyahoga County Medical Examiner and the DNA Doe Project began exhuming and DNA-testing unidentified victims, funded by a single donor. No identifications had been announced as of August 2026.

Three men, no conviction

Same case
Eli’s reading

Say plainly that nobody knows.

Dolezal’s confession is compromised, Johnson’s link is thin and secondhand, and Sweeney has no charge and no forensic tie. Opportunity and profile are not evidence, and with the records gone there is no floor to check against.

Simone’s reading

Read the pattern, not the paper trail.

Dr. Francis Sweeney failed two polygraphs from the era’s leading examiner, had field-surgery training, a documented history of alcoholism and psychiatric breakdown, and an office next to the morgue. None of it is forensic. She thinks the read is right anyway.

Zero convictions against any of the three men for these murders, and a failed polygraph is not physical evidence. The story of decades of threatening postcards from Sweeney to Ness, and the claim that Ness avoided prosecuting him to protect his political standing, are each single-source.

1934 to 2026
  1. 1934SEPTEMBERThe first victims are found in and around Kingsbury Run. Some sources count the “Lady of the Lake” among them.
  2. 1934–38THE KILLINGSAt least 12 official victims. Only two are ever named, Edward Andrassy and Florence Polillo, both by fingerprints.
  3. 1938AUGUSTThe last of the official victims is found. The killer is never identified.
  4. 1938AUG. 18Ness, who personally interviewed Dr. Francis Sweeney, orders a raid that burns the Kingsbury Run shantytowns: 35 officers, 11 squad cars, 63 men detained.
  5. 1939DOLEZALFrank Dolezal is arrested, confesses to killing Florence Polillo in self-defense, then recants, claiming coercion. He is found hanged in custody with six broken ribs. His death is ruled a suicide.
  6. 1944JOHNSONWillie Johnson is executed for a separate 1942 murder with a similar pattern. He is never conclusively linked to Kingsbury Run.
  7. 2024AUGUSTThe Cuyahoga County Medical Examiner and the DNA Doe Project begin exhuming and DNA-testing unidentified victims.
  8. 2026TODAYThe case is officially unsolved. No identifications have been announced.
