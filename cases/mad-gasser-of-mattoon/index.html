September 5, 1944 · Mattoon, Illinois

The Mad Gasser of Mattoon

Content note This case involves a wartime panic, reports of chemical injury and a town that never agreed on what happened. No one was ever charged, so this page keeps to what the public record supports.

In September 1944 a small Illinois town reported a mysterious gas attacker. Nobody was ever arrested. One porch left physical evidence behind. This is what Eli and Simone argued over.

September 5, 1944

The porch

Beulah Cordes picked up a cloth on her porch, smelled it, and her lips swelled and her mouth and throat began to burn.

  • The clothBalled up on the Cordes porch
  • Also foundA skeleton key and a tube of lipstick, neither belonging to the household
  • The linkNo connection between the three objects has ever been established
  • Earliest reportThe Raef household, night of Aug. 31 to Sept. 1, not public until later
  1. 01 / 04 · KEARNEY

    On Sept. 1, 1944, Aline Kearney reported a sweet odor in her bedroom and numb legs. Her husband Bert saw a man outside the window. The Journal Gazette ran it on page one.

  2. 02 / 04 · THE STUDY

    Donald Johnson’s 1945 field study called that front page the “index” event and read the episode as mass hysteria.

  3. 03 / 04 · THE OFFICIAL

    Public health commissioner Thomas Wright: “There is no doubt that a gas maniac exists… But many of the reported attacks are nothing more than hysteria.”

  4. 04 / 04 · THE CHIEF

    On Sept. 12, Chief C. E. Cole floated an industrial source. The plant denied it, and reports fell sharply within days.

Eli’s reading

Mass hysteria

Reports climbed with the press coverage and collapsed after the police chief publicly doubted them. Wartime fear was the fuel and the front page was the match.

Simone’s reading

Something happened on that porch

The media and a real physical incident can both be true. Hysteria explains the volume, but it has never explained the Cordes burns or the objects recovered.

The Farley Llewellyn theory, from a self-published 2003 book, is single-source. Wikipedia’s editors flag its sourcing as unreliable.

Two weeks in September 1944

Aug. 31 to Sept. 18
  1. 1944AUG. 31–SEPT. 1The Raef household reports a sweet smell, nausea and partial paralysis. It does not become public until later.
  2. 1944SEPT. 1Aline Kearney’s report runs on the front page of the Mattoon Journal Gazette.
  3. 1944SEPT. 5Beulah Cordes finds the cloth, a key and a lipstick tube on her porch.
  4. 1944SEPT. 6–12Reports accelerate. Illinois State Police and FBI agents get involved.
  5. 1944SEPT. 11A report describes a short, heavyset suspect in women’s shoes. It was never corroborated.
  6. 1944SEPT. 12Chief Cole floats an industrial explanation. The plant denies it.
  7. 1944SEPT. 18Time magazine runs “The Mad Anesthetist” nationally.
