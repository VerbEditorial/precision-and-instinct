January 1949 · Cottage City, Maryland & St. Louis, Missouri

The Real Exorcist: Roland Doe

Disputed The boy at the centre of the case, known only by the pseudonym “Roland Doe,” died in 2020. Whether he was possessed or a grieving, distressed child has never been settled, and there is no documentary or forensic proof either way.

A 13-year-old boy, a Ouija board given to him by an aunt, and weeks of exorcism rites led by Catholic priests in St. Louis. This is what Eli and Simone argued over.

One record, found by accident

The priest’s diary

Most of what is known about the case traces back to a single contemporaneous record kept by Fr. Raymond Bishop.

  • WrittenDuring the exorcism, 1949
  • Left inA dresser drawer, in a room later sealed
  • Found1978, by a demolition workman
  • Made publicThomas B. Allen’s Possessed, 1993
  1. 01 / 04 · THE BOARD

    The trouble began shortly after the death of an aunt who had given him a Ouija board.

  2. 02 / 04 · THE PASTOR

    The family’s Lutheran pastor saw some of it himself, then advised them to consult a Catholic priest.

  3. 03 / 04 · THE WELTS

    Welts reportedly spelled out words, including “LOUIS.” That detail traces to a single line of accounts.

  4. 04 / 04 · THE WATER

    He reportedly writhed under holy water. The famous blind test with plain water isn’t in the better sources.

Two explanations

Same record
The skeptical reading

A grieving, distressed child.

Thomas Allen, whose book is the case’s main source, concluded the boy’s behaviour was distress expressed physically and read through a religious frame.

The believers’ reading

Too many witnesses to dismiss.

A Lutheran pastor and two Catholic priests, in two states and weeks apart, described the same specific physical claims.

Neither side has documentary or forensic proof.
