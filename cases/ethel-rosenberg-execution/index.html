June 19, 1953 · Sing Sing Prison, Ossining, New York

Ethel Rosenberg Execution: The Typewriter

Content note This case involves the death penalty, a graphic account of an execution, and Cold War political and antisemitic persecution. Ethel Rosenberg’s two sons are still living and have spent decades contesting the government’s case against their mother, so this page keeps to the public record.

The only American civilians executed for espionage in peacetime. Julius’s role is documented. Ethel’s is the live argument, and it rests on a typewriter story that first surfaced ten days before trial. This is what Eli and Simone argued over.

Arrested three weeks apart

Husband and wife

Julius was arrested first. Ethel followed three weeks later, without a comparable chain of evidence leading to her.

  • July 17, 1950Julius is arrested
  • August 11, 1950Ethel is arrested
  • The chainKlaus Fuchs, to courier Harry Gold, to her brother David Greenglass
  • The chargeConspiracy to commit espionage
  1. 01 / 04 · THE TESTIMONY

    At trial, David Greenglass said his sister Ethel typed up his handwritten notes for the Soviets.

  2. 02 / 04 · TEN DAYS OUT

    His earlier statements left it out. It first appears in a revised statement about ten days before the trial opened on March 6, 1951. Grand jury testimony released in 2017 never mentions it.

  3. 03 / 04 · THE RETRACTION

    In 2001 Greenglass told New York Times reporter Sam Roberts he never actually witnessed Ethel typing, and that he thought his wife Ruth likely did it.

  4. 04 / 04 · THE CABLES

    Ethel appears in decrypted Soviet traffic but never got a codename. Julius appears as “Antenna,” later “Liberal.”

Two readings of Ethel

Same file
Eli’s reading

Julius is closed. Ethel is thin.

The cables and Sobell’s 2008 confession settle Julius. Ethel’s file is one cable with no codename and a typewriter story that surfaced ten days before trial and was recanted decades later. That isn’t proof beyond a reasonable doubt.

Simone’s reading

A lever, used until it killed her.

What the government had on Ethel when it chose to prosecute her wasn’t evidence of a spy. Julius was the target, and the threat to his wife was the pressure. Backing off would have meant admitting it was empty.

Alexander Vassiliev’s KGB notebooks, published in 2009, say Ethel knew of her husband’s work. They don’t show her acting as an agent the way Julius did.

From two arrests to a retraction

1950 to 2014
  1. 1950JULY 17Julius is arrested. The trail ran from Klaus Fuchs, caught by British intelligence, to courier Harry Gold to David Greenglass, Ethel’s brother.
  2. 1950AUGUST 11Ethel is arrested three weeks later, without a comparable chain of evidence against her.
  3. 1951MARCH 6The trial opens. About ten days earlier, Greenglass’s revised statement first claims Ethel typed his notes.
  4. 1951APRILFBI Director J. Edgar Hoover recommends against the death penalty for Ethel. The prosecution team itself is divided.
  5. 1953JUNE 19Both are executed at Sing Sing, moved up to 8 p.m. so it ends before the Jewish Sabbath. Julius dies after three shocks. Ethel takes five, with smoke reported rising from her head.
  6. 1995VENONADeclassified Soviet cables show Julius as “Antenna” and later “Liberal.” Ethel appears but is never given a codename.
  7. 2001THE RETRACTIONGreenglass tells Sam Roberts of The New York Times that he never witnessed Ethel typing.
  8. 2008–14THE AFTERMATHMorton Sobell confesses his and Julius’s guilt in 2008. Greenglass dies in 2014, and the Meeropol brothers ask Presidents Obama and Biden to exonerate Ethel. Neither acts.
