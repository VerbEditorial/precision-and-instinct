July 30, 1975 · Machus Red Fox, Bloomfield Township, Michigan

The Disappearance of Jimmy Hoffa

Open Case No one has ever been charged in Jimmy Hoffa’s disappearance, and every person ever publicly named as a participant is now dead. In July 2026 Hoffa’s son and daughter asked the FBI to release its files and name those responsible. This page does not resolve that dispute, and neither did the episode. Every claim is tiered by how well established it is.

On July 30, 1975, the most powerful labor leader in America left home for a lunch meeting at a Detroit-area steakhouse and was never seen again. Fifty-one years later the case is not short of confident theories. It is short of proof anyone can stand behind. The hosts split on how much weight the sworn record deserves.

July 30, 1975

The parking lot

Hoffa left his Lake Orion home for an expected meeting at the Machus Red Fox. He called his wife from the restaurant that afternoon to say the man he was there to meet had not shown, and that he would be home by 4. His car was found unattended in the lot the next morning. He was never seen again.

  • The meetingAnthony Giacalone, an associate of Teamsters boss Anthony Provenzano
  • The callHoffa phones home from the restaurant: the man he came to meet has not shown
  • The carFound unattended in the Red Fox parking lot the next morning
  • The disputeHoffa is fighting to return to the Teamsters, against a restriction that runs to 1980
  1. 01 / 04 · 1975, THE MERCURY

    Three weeks after Hoffa vanished, police dogs alerted to his scent in a 1975 Mercury Marquis that his foster son, Charles “Chuckie” O’Brien, had borrowed that day. A shotgun and loose ammunition were found in the car.

  2. 02 / 04 · 1975–76, TESTIMONY AND MEMO

    In December 1975 a federal investigator testified under oath that three men tied to Provenzano took part in the abduction and murder, relying substantially on informant Ralph Picardo. None was ever charged. The FBI’s 56-page “Hoffex” memo in 1976 concluded organized crime ordered the killing.

  3. 03 / 04 · 2001, THE HAIR

    FBI testing matched a hair from Hoffa’s own hairbrush to one found in the Mercury’s back seat, with the Bureau’s own caveat that he could have ridden in the car on another day.

  4. 04 / 04 · 1975–2022, THE SEARCHES

    A Waterford Township site, a horse farm, a Roseville driveway, an Oakland County property and a New Jersey landfill all ended with no remains and nothing “of evidentiary value.” Hoffa was declared legally dead in 1982.

A sworn record, or a story with confidence

Same case
Eli’s reading

The sworn record is the closest thing there is.

The dogs, the 1975 testimony, the Hoffex memo and the hair all point the same direction. Eli grants that the informant had an incentive but will not call his account worthless. It still is not a conviction, and nobody has earned the right to call the case closed.

Simone’s reading

Confidence has always outrun the proof.

Four threads that trace back to the same working theory are what a story sounds like once everyone has decided how it ends. Two credentialed insiders publicly doubt the testimony naming Gabriel Briguglio. From a movie in 2019 to a radar reading in 2020, confidence kept getting ahead of evidence.

Who ordered or carried out the killing is disputed. Frank Sheeran’s deathbed confession is single-source and challenged by reporters and legal scholars, whose review found the blood evidence at the house he named did not match Hoffa. Richard Kuklinski’s jailhouse claims were called a hoax by a former FBI agent. Michael Franzese’s on-camera claim to know the shooter was never backed by a name. Fox Nation’s radar reading of barrel-shaped metal was one production team’s equipment read, and the FBI’s 2021–22 dig found nothing of evidentiary value. Gabriel Briguglio, named in the 1975 testimony, denied it until he died in November 2025.
