September 28 – October 1, 1982 · Chicago area, Illinois

The Chicago Tylenol Murders

Open Case No one has ever been charged with the 1982 poisonings. James W. Lewis, the man most associated with the case, was convicted only of an unrelated extortion attempt and died in 2023 still denying any involvement in the murders. The case remains officially open. This page lists no victims’ images, only objects and places, and every claim is tiered by how well established it is.

Seven people died in four days in 1982 after taking Extra-Strength Tylenol that someone had laced with cyanide and put back on store shelves. The recall and the tamper-proof bottle came within weeks. The killer was never charged. The hosts split on what the man most associated with the case really tells us.

September 28 – October 1, 1982

The shelf

The poison did not come from the factory. Someone bought bottles off store shelves, opened them, laced the capsules with potassium cyanide and returned them to shelves at several Chicago-area retailers. Seven people bought something ordinary and none of them knew.

  • The productExtra-Strength Tylenol capsules laced with potassium cyanide
  • The storesJewel, Osco, Walgreens, Dominick’s and Frank’s Finer Foods
  • The bottle that broke it openFour of the 44 capsules left in the Janus family’s bottle carried nearly triple a fatal dose
  • The recallAbout 31 million bottles recalled nationwide on October 5, 1982
  1. 01 / 04 · SEPT. 28 – OCT. 1, 1982, THE POISONINGS

    Seven people died in the Chicago area after taking capsules from tampered bottles. A public health nurse noticed six capsules missing from one family’s bottle, and a medical examiner’s investigator caught an almond-like odor.

  2. 02 / 04 · OCT. 1, 1982, THE LETTER

    A letter signed “Robert Richardson” demanded $1 million to “stop the killing.” It held technical detail about the tampering that had not been made public. It was later tied to James W. Lewis.

  3. 03 / 04 · 1982–1984, THE CHARGES

    Lewis was arrested December 13, 1982. In June 1984 he was convicted of extortion under the Hobbs Act and sentenced to 10 years. He was never charged with the murders.

  4. 04 / 04 · 2009–2023, AFTER

    Documents unsealed in 2009 show federal investigators privately concluded Lewis was responsible but lacked evidence to charge him. Lewis and Roger Arnold both tested negative for a DNA match in 2010. Lewis died July 9, 2023.

Close enough to cash in, or not enough to charge

Same case
Eli’s reading

Belief was never the same as proof.

No fingerprints, no DNA, and nothing places Lewis in Chicago that week or puts his hands on a bottle. The federal conclusion is a well-informed read, but it is still not evidence. His story about the letter moved over forty years, yet it never became a confession.

Simone’s reading

He knew something the public didn’t.

A ransom letter with operational detail days after the poisonings is not a coincidence she can explain away. Add a federal conclusion that sat unactioned for decades and the pattern of aliases, and she will not concede he is innocent. Degraded evidence, she argues, is not the same as no evidence.

Whether Lewis was the killer is still unresolved. Accounts differ on whether Theresa Janus died on September 29 or 30. Roger Arnold, an early person of interest, was never charged in the Tylenol case. The powder found in his home tested as potassium carbonate, not cyanide, and most sources treat him as a discredited lead. The FBI’s 2011 request for Ted Kaczynski’s DNA rested only on a suburb and is treated as a thin, largely dismissed thread. The episode does not make the allegations about Johnson & Johnson that the research flagged as single-source. A developing lead surfaced in September 2026: Idaho authorities identified a chemist who died of cyanide poisoning in a Boise church in December 1982 and said he may be connected. The two lead news accounts disagree on several specifics, and no agency calls him a suspect. The clips leave it out because it is still moving.
