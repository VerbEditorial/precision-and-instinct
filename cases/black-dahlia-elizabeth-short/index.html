January 15, 1947 · Leimert Park, Los Angeles

The Black Dahlia: Elizabeth Short

Unsolved Elizabeth Short was killed in 1947. The case is still formally open and no one has ever been charged. The two suspect theories discussed here are circumstantial and disputed, and neither is presented as settled.

In January 1947 a mother walking with her child found Elizabeth Short in a vacant lot in Leimert Park. No one has ever been charged. This is what Eli and Simone argued over.

Mailed to a newsroom

The package nobody could trace

About nine days after the body was found, a manila envelope arrived at the Los Angeles Examiner. Whoever sent it had wiped everything down with gasoline.

  • ArrivedAround January 24, 1947, at the Los Angeles Examiner
  • InsideHer birth certificate, business cards, photographs and an address book
  • The bookEmbossed with the name Mark Hansen
  • The printsPartial prints recovered, later compromised in transit to the FBI
  1. 01 / 04 · THE LOT

    Her body was found on January 15, 1947, in a vacant lot on South Norton Avenue. No blood was found at the scene, which had been scrubbed clean.

  2. 02 / 04 · THE PRINTS

    Her fingerprints from a 1943 arrest in Santa Barbara are what let the FBI identify her.

  3. 03 / 04 · THE LETTERS

    Taunting letters followed on January 26 and 29, including a failed attempt to arrange a “surrender.”

  4. 04 / 04 · THE CONFESSIONS

    About 60 people confessed in the first investigation, and the count passed 500 over the decades. More than 150 men were interviewed.

Two theories, neither charged

Same case
Theory one

Dr. George Hodel.

A Los Angeles physician tried in 1949 for allegedly molesting his 14-year-old daughter, and acquitted. A 1950 wiretap caught him saying that even if he had killed the Black Dahlia, no one could prove it. His son Steve, a retired LAPD homicide detective, built the case against him.

Theory two

Leslie Dillon and Mark Hansen.

Piu Eatwell’s 2017 book Black Dahlia, Red Rose names a bellhop and former mortician’s assistant, acting for a nightclub owner who had pursued and been rejected by Short. The Aster Motel room where the killing is said to have happened was reportedly found covered in blood the day after, and cleaned up.

LAPD investigated both men and never brought charges. No source treats either theory as settled.
