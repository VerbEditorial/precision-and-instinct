March 1970 · Los Angeles and Southern California

The Confessions of Mack Ray Edwards

Content note This case involves the murders of children and a suicide in custody. No victim is shown and graphic detail is kept out of this page. Edwards claimed many more victims than he was convicted of. Those claims were never charged and the remains have never been found, and this page does not resolve them.

In March 1970 a heavy-equipment operator surrendered at a Los Angeles police station and put a loaded handgun on the desk. He was convicted of three murders and claimed many more. Searches for some of the others in 1970, 2008 and 2011 found nothing. The hosts agree on the three. They split on the rest.

1953 to 1970

Three charged, many claimed

Mack Ray Edwards pleaded guilty to three counts of first-degree murder and three counts of kidnapping and was sentenced to death. He had first confessed to six killings. Later reports put his claim at eighteen to twenty.

  • The convictionsThree murders: 1953, 1968 and 1969
  • The pleaGuilty on all counts, sentenced to death
  • The surrenderLAPD, March 5–6, 1970, a loaded handgun on the desk
  • The claimsSix at first, later reported as 18 to 20
  1. 01 / 04 · THE JOB

    Edwards worked for years as a heavy-equipment operator and crew chief on California highway construction projects.

  2. 02 / 04 · THE ONE THAT MATCHED

    The remains of Stella Darlene Nolan, killed in 1953, were recovered on an embankment tied to Santa Ana Freeway construction.

  3. 03 / 04 · THE EMPTY SEARCHES

    In 1970 Edwards directed investigators to a site where he said he had left two children’s remains from 1956. None were found. Excavations near a 23 Freeway exit in Moorpark in October 2008 and beside the Winchester Canyon overpass in Goleta in June 2011 also found nothing.

  4. 04 / 04 · WHAT THAT MEANS

    The empty searches don’t disprove Edwards’s claims, but they don’t confirm them either.

The other names

Same case
Eli’s reading

Unproven is where I stop.

Where Edwards’s account could be checked against something physical, it held up. Where it couldn’t, three searches came back empty. A detailed, shifting body count from one source is not evidence on its own.

Simone’s reading

No body is not the same as no killing.

Edwards had years of unsupervised access to open construction ground, his one corroborated victim was found on that kind of site, and she sees no motive to pad the number after his sentence. She credits most of what he claimed.

Only three cases were ever charged. The Roger Madison details, the line about no one tearing up a freeway, and the eighteen-to-twenty total each rest on a single account with no primary document behind it.

1953 to 2011
  1. 1953NOLANStella Darlene Nolan is killed. Her remains are later recovered on an embankment tied to Santa Ana Freeway construction. It is one of the three cases ever charged.
  2. 1956BAKER AND HOWELLDon Baker and Brenda Howell, Edwards’s sister-in-law, disappear near Morris Dam. In 1970 Edwards directs investigators to a site. Nothing is found.
  3. 1961PRICERamona Price goes missing in Santa Barbara. Edwards had worked on a nearby overpass. No charge is ever brought.
  4. 1968–69ROCHET AND TODDGary Rochet and Donald Allen Todd are killed. These are the other two cases that lead to charges.
  5. 1970MARCH 5–6The day after a botched attempt on three girls in Sylmar, all of whom survive, Edwards surrenders to LAPD with a loaded handgun.
  6. 1971OCT. 30Edwards hangs himself in his cell at San Quentin after two earlier attempts.
  7. 2008OCTOBERLAPD, Ventura County deputies and the FBI dig for about five days near a 23 Freeway exit in Moorpark, looking for Roger Dale Madison. They find nothing.
  8. 2011JUNESanta Barbara police search near the Winchester Canyon overpass in Goleta. Cadaver dogs alert, but nothing is found.
