1 December 1948 · Somerton Park beach, Adelaide

The Somerton Man

Open Case A 2022 DNA study named the man as Carl "Charles" Webb. South Australia Police finished their own DNA analysis in December 2023, but the Coroner has not issued a formal finding. How he died is still unknown.

John Lyons and his companions found him reclined against the seawall as if asleep, a half-smoked cigarette resting on his collar. This is what Eli and Simone argued over.

  1. 01 / 04 · ON THE BODY

    No hat. No wallet. No identification of any kind.

  2. 02 / 04 · THE CLOTHES

    Every label had been removed from his clothing.

  3. 03 / 04 · THE SHOES

    Clean, and freshly polished.

  4. 04 / 04 · THE AUTOPSY

    The pathologist believed it wasn't natural. Period tests found no poison.

Checked in the day before

The suitcase

Handed in on 30 November 1948. Recovered on 14 January 1949.

← All case filesCase No. 012 · 2010 · Status: Open

23 August 2010 · Pimlico, central London

Spy in a Bag: The Death of Gareth Williams

Open Case A 2021–2023 Metropolitan Police forensic review found no new DNA and no new leads. Police say he most likely died alone, but that the case “cannot be resolved beyond reasonable doubt.” His family rejects the accident finding.

Gareth Williams, a 31-year-old GCHQ mathematician seconded to MI6, was found inside a padlocked holdall in the bath of his flat. This is what Eli and Simone argued over.

Nobody raised the alarm

The missing week

Eight days passed between the last sighting and the day police opened his door.

  • Last confirmed sighting, on CCTV15 Aug, about 3pm
  • Expected at workNo alarm raised
  • Police check the flat23 Aug
  1. 01 / 04 · THE BAG

    A red holdall, padlocked, in the bath of his flat.

  2. 02 / 04 · THE FLAT

    No forced entry. No fingerprints. No sign the flat had been cleaned.

  3. 03 / 04 · THE DNA

    Traces from two unknown people on the bag, too degraded to build a profile.

  4. 04 / 04 · THE TEST

    At the 2012 inquest, escape artists made about 400 attempts to lock themselves into the same type of bag. None managed it.

Two official answers

Same evidence
May 2012 · The inquest

“Likely” unlawfully killed.

Coroner Dr Fiona Wilcox recorded a narrative verdict of death by unnatural causes, and criticised MI6 for delaying its cooperation with police.

November 2013 · Metropolitan Police

“Probably an accident.”

No other person likely present, while acknowledging “evidential contradictions and gaps in our understanding.”

Nobody has ever been charged.

Two verdicts, still no answer

2010 to now
  1. 201015 AUGCCTV records the last confirmed sighting of him, at about 3pm.
  2. 201023 AUGPolice find him in a padlocked holdall in the bath of his Pimlico flat.
  3. 2012MAYThe inquest finds it likely he was unlawfully killed, and criticises MI6.
  4. 2013NOVEMBERThe Metropolitan Police conclude it was probably an accident. His family rejects this.
  5. 2021NEW REVIEWAdvances in DNA science prompt a fresh forensic review.
  6. 2023REVIEW ENDSNo new DNA, no new leads. The case “cannot be resolved beyond reasonable doubt.”

Two readings

No winner
Precision · Eli Marchetti

“Four hundred attempts by trained professionals is data.”

  • “Theoretically possible is not the same as demonstrated.”
  • “That’s an intelligence agency slow-walking a murder investigation into one of its own people.”
  • “Every explanation on the table is, on its own terms, unlikely.”
Instinct · Simone Valdez

“Undetectable assassinations are rare precisely because they’re hard to pull off cleanly.”

  • “The people whose entire job is noticing things — didn’t notice him.”
  • “An escape-artist trial under studio conditions, with cameras and observers and no adrenaline, is not automatically the same as knowing what one specific, motivated person could do alone…”
  • “Or it’s an institution correcting an inquest verdict that leaned harder on ‘impossible’ than the evidence actually supported.”

In their own words, from Episode 12.

No verdict.

  1. Could one person lock that bag from the inside, alone, on one particular night?
  2. Whose DNA is on the bag?
  3. How does an intelligence agency not notice one of its own is missing for over a week?

← All case filesCase No. 002 · 1949 · Status: Disputed

January 1949 · Cottage City, Maryland & St. Louis, Missouri

The Real Exorcist

Disputed The boy at the centre of the case, known only by the pseudonym “Roland Doe,” died in 2020. Whether he was possessed or a grieving, distressed child has never been settled, and there is no documentary or forensic proof either way.

A 13-year-old boy, a Ouija board given to him by an aunt, and weeks of exorcism rites led by Catholic priests in St. Louis. This is what Eli and Simone argued over.

One record, found by accident

The priest’s diary

Most of what is known about the case traces back to a single contemporaneous record kept by Fr. Raymond Bishop.

  • WrittenDuring the exorcism, 1949
  • Left inA dresser drawer, in a room later sealed
  • Found1978, by a demolition workman
  • Made publicThomas B. Allen’s Possessed, 1993
  1. 01 / 04 · THE BOARD

    The trouble began shortly after the death of an aunt who had given him a Ouija board.

  2. 02 / 04 · THE PASTOR

    The family’s Lutheran pastor saw some of it himself, then advised them to consult a Catholic priest.

  3. 03 / 04 · THE WELTS

    Welts reportedly spelled out words, including “LOUIS.” That detail traces to a single line of accounts.

  4. 04 / 04 · THE WATER

    He reportedly writhed under holy water. The famous blind test with plain water isn’t in the better sources.

Two explanations

Same record
The skeptical reading

A grieving, distressed child.

Thomas Allen, whose book is the case’s main source, concluded the boy’s behaviour was distress expressed physically and read through a religious frame.

The believers’ reading

Too many witnesses to dismiss.

A Lutheran pastor and two Catholic priests, in two states and weeks apart, described the same specific physical claims.

Neither side has documentary or forensic proof.

From a Maryland house to a Hollywood film

1949 to 2021
  1. 1949JANUARYDisturbing behaviour begins in Cottage City, Maryland, soon after an aunt’s death.
  2. 1949ST. LOUISFr. Bishop and Fr. Bowdern lead the exorcism, with the archbishop’s permission.
  3. 1949THE PRESSWilliam Peter Blatty, a Georgetown student, reads about the case.
  4. 1971THE NOVELBlatty publishes The Exorcist. The film follows in 1973.
  5. 1978DEMOLITIONA workman finds Fr. Bishop’s diary in a sealed room at Alexian Brothers Hospital.
  6. 1993THE BOOKThomas B. Allen publishes Possessed, built on the diary.
  7. 2005MARCHWalter Halloran, the last surviving priest from the case, dies at 83.
  8. 2020MAYThe man known as “Roland Doe” dies at 86. His identity is confirmed in 2021.

Two readings

No winner
Precision · Eli Marchetti

“That’s not independent verification, that’s two belief systems agreeing with each other.”

  • “I’m not calling it a fabrication. I’m calling it a single, interested source.”
  • “A boy thrashing hard enough to shift a mattress isn’t the same as a mattress moving on its own.”
  • “I still don’t think either of us actually knows what was in that room.”
Instinct · Simone Valdez

“You can’t interpret a mattress shaking on its own, Eli.”

  • “If the standards were weak, that’s also a reason not to fully trust a clean psychological explanation either.”
  • “It’s the most honest witness we have in this whole case.”
  • “At some point, everyone happened to imagine the same thing is a bigger leap of faith than the one they made.”

In their own words, from Episode 02.

No verdict.

  1. What did the adults in that room actually see?
  2. How far can a case be trusted when one priest’s diary is its main source?
  3. Where does the real case end and the novel and film begin?

← All case filesCase No. 003 · 1992 · Status: Unsolved

June 6, 1992 · Springfield, Missouri

The Springfield Three

Unsolved This is a real disappearance that has never been solved. Suzie Streeter, Stacy McCall and Sherrill Levitt have never been found, and their families are still looking for answers.

Three women, one house, one night in June 1992. In the morning their cars, purses and keys were still there, and they were gone. This is what Eli and Simone argued over.

Heard, reported, then erased

The message nobody can hear

A message on Sherrill Levitt’s answering machine struck the people who heard it as strange enough to report to police. It was accidentally erased before investigators could listen to it.

  • Last callAround 11:15 PM, about painting a chest of drawers
  • The messageDescribed as strange or unsettling
  • ReportedTo police, by those who heard it
  • NowAccidentally erased, content unknown
  1. 01 / 04 · THE CARS

    All three women’s cars were still in the driveway the next morning.

  2. 02 / 04 · THE BELONGINGS

    Purses, keys, jewelry and other personal items were left inside the house.

  3. 03 / 04 · THE HOUSE

    There was no sign of forced entry and no sign of a violent struggle.

  4. 04 / 04 · THE PORCH LIGHT

    The glass shade was shattered, with glass on the ground. Friends swept it away before police could treat it as evidence.

Two readings of an empty house

Same evidence
Eli’s reading

Unsolvable, for now.

No blood, no struggle, no remains and no confession that holds up. With nothing physical pointing anywhere, the honest answer may be that the evidence can’t solve it.

Simone’s reading

The shape of an abduction.

A broken porch light and three sets of belongings nobody would leave behind are circumstantial evidence in themselves. Nobody walked out of that house by choice.

Every source treats this as unresolved. None claims a settled explanation.

Thirty years of tips

1978 to 2007
  1. 1978FLORIDASharon Zellers, 19, is murdered near Walt Disney World. Robert Craig Cox is convicted in 1988, then freed when the conviction is overturned.
  2. 1992JUNE 6Suzie and Stacy graduate from Kickapoo High School. By morning, both of them and Sherrill Levitt are gone.
  3. 1993THE INFORMANTSteven Garrison, arrested on a weapons charge, begins cooperating with police. His tip leads to a rural search.
  4. 1995TEXASCox is arrested for an armed robbery and sentenced to 30 years.
  5. 1996JANUARYIn prison, Cox says he knows the women are dead and will never be found.
  6. 1997DECLARED DEADSherrill and Suzie are declared legally dead. Stacy is declared dead in a separate petition.
  7. 2003CASSVILLEA backhoe dig finds a trace of blood and a green vehicle fragment. The blood test is inconclusive.
  8. 2007THE GARAGEGround-penetrating radar flags three anomalies under a hospital garage. Police decline to act: it was built in 1993.

Two readings

No winner
Precision · Eli Marchetti

“Absence of an explanation isn’t evidence of a specific one, Simone.”

  • “We’re building theory around a piece of evidence nobody alive has actually heard in over 30 years.”
  • “Strangers can’t close a case, though. Only evidence can, and there isn’t any.”
  • “Sometimes, the honest answer is that a case can be genuinely unsolvable with the evidence available.”
Instinct · Simone Valdez

“Glass on the ground isn’t how a bulb dies of natural causes, Eli.”

  • “Being looked at isn’t the same as being cleared.”
  • “That’s not a denial, Eli. That’s a man enjoying being asked.”
  • “If you asked me what I actually believe happened in that house, I don’t think they walked out on their own.”

In their own words, from Episode 03.

No verdict.

  2. What was on the message nobody can hear anymore?
  3. Did anyone questioned in this case know more than they said?

← All case filesCase No. 004 · 1947 · Status: Unsolved

January 15, 1947 · Leimert Park, Los Angeles

The Black Dahlia

Unsolved Elizabeth Short was killed in 1947. The case is still formally open and no one has ever been charged. The two suspect theories discussed here are circumstantial and disputed, and neither is presented as settled.

In January 1947 a mother walking with her child found Elizabeth Short in a vacant lot in Leimert Park. No one has ever been charged. This is what Eli and Simone argued over.

Mailed to a newsroom

The package nobody could trace

About nine days after the body was found, a manila envelope arrived at the Los Angeles Examiner. Whoever sent it had wiped everything down with gasoline.

  • ArrivedAround January 24, 1947, at the Los Angeles Examiner
  • InsideHer birth certificate, business cards, photographs and an address book
  • The bookEmbossed with the name Mark Hansen
  • The printsPartial prints recovered, later compromised in transit to the FBI
  1. 01 / 04 · THE LOT

    Her body was found on January 15, 1947, in a vacant lot on South Norton Avenue. No blood was found at the scene, which had been scrubbed clean.

  2. 02 / 04 · THE PRINTS

    Her fingerprints from a 1943 arrest in Santa Barbara are what let the FBI identify her.

  3. 03 / 04 · THE LETTERS

    Taunting letters followed on January 26 and 29, including a failed attempt to arrange a “surrender.”

  4. 04 / 04 · THE CONFESSIONS

    About 60 people confessed in the first investigation, and the count passed 500 over the decades. More than 150 men were interviewed.

Two theories, neither charged

Same case
Theory one

Dr. George Hodel.

A Los Angeles physician tried in 1949 for allegedly molesting his 14-year-old daughter, and acquitted. A 1950 wiretap caught him saying that even if he had killed the Black Dahlia, no one could prove it. His son Steve, a retired LAPD homicide detective, built the case against him.

Theory two

Leslie Dillon and Mark Hansen.

Piu Eatwell’s 2017 book Black Dahlia, Red Rose names a bellhop and former mortician’s assistant, acting for a nightclub owner who had pursued and been rejected by Short. The Aster Motel room where the killing is said to have happened was reportedly found covered in blood the day after, and cleaned up.

LAPD investigated both men and never brought charges. No source treats either theory as settled.

From Boston to a cold case

1924 to 2017
  1. 1924BOSTONElizabeth Short is born in Boston.
  2. 1930THE BRIDGEHer father’s car is found abandoned on a bridge and the family assumes he died. He later resurfaces alive in California, and she moves west in 1942 partly to reconnect with him.
  3. 1943SEPTEMBERShe is arrested for underage drinking at a bar in Santa Barbara. The booking photo becomes the image the world later associates with the case.
  4. 1947JANUARY 15A mother walking with her child finds her body in a vacant lot in Leimert Park.
  5. 1947JANUARY 24A manila envelope wiped with gasoline arrives at the Los Angeles Examiner. Taunting letters follow on January 26 and 29.
  6. 1950THE WIRETAPHodel, acquitted a year earlier, has his home bugged by the DA’s office and is recorded saying no one could prove he killed the Black Dahlia.
  7. 2003THE ARCHIVEThe wiretap file is rediscovered in the DA’s archives.
  8. 2017THE BOOKPiu Eatwell publishes Black Dahlia, Red Rose, naming a rival theory.

Two readings

No winner
Precision · Eli Marchetti

“Patterns aren’t verdicts.”

  • “That’s a theory, Simone, not a fact.”
  • “A killer taunting the press doesn’t hand you a killer’s name.”
  • “It’s a reason to distrust the whole process equally.”
Instinct · Simone Valdez

“That’s not one coincidence, Eli. That’s a pattern.”

  • “That’s not just violence, Eli. That’s a signature.”
  • “Everyone in this story wanted to be part of telling it.”
  • “Whoever did this, in every serious version of this story, knew her.”

In their own words, from Episode 04.

No verdict.

  1. Why would a killer mail the evidence to a newsroom instead of destroying it?
  2. Is the Hodel wiretap evidence, or a man taunting police he knew were listening?
  3. Can a case built on competing theories ever be closed?

← All case filesCase No. 005 · 1957 · Status: Open case

February 25, 1957 · Fox Chase, Philadelphia

The Boy in the Box

Open case This is an open, unsolved child-homicide case. Police have said they have suspicions, but no suspect has ever been publicly named, and this page does not speculate about who it might be. Joseph’s parents have died. He has living half-siblings, so this page keeps to the public record.

A boy found in a cardboard box in 1957, unnamed for sixty-five years. In 2022 he finally got his name back. No one has been charged. This is what Eli and Simone argued over.

Unnamed for 65 years

Two headstones

He was buried in 1957 under a donated stone for an unknown boy. A new one, with his name added, was dedicated in 2023.

  • 1957Potter’s field near Dunks Ferry, Northeast Philadelphia
  • PallbearersHomicide detectives
  • The first stone“Heavenly Father, Bless This Unknown Boy”
  • 2023Ivy Hill Cemetery: “America’s Unknown Child,” with his name added
  1. 01 / 04 · THE FIRST TRY

    A 1998 exhumation tried to pull mitochondrial DNA from a tooth. There wasn’t enough to identify him, and he was reburied, still unnamed.

  2. 02 / 04 · THE SECOND TRY

    In 2019 a second exhumation recovered a fuller sample. Forensic genealogist Colleen Fitzpatrick described the DNA as “like confetti.”

  3. 03 / 04 · THE COUSINS

    Genealogist Misty Gillis built a family tree from cousin matches on GEDmatch, “like a big Sudoku puzzle.”

  4. 04 / 04 · THE NAME

    In December 2022 police announced he was Joseph Augustus Zarelli, born January 13, 1953.

Two readings of a name

Same case
Eli’s reading

DNA broke it open.

Decades of tips and theories went nowhere because none of them was proof. One genetic-genealogy effort, built on cousins nobody knew about, did what those leads couldn’t. Proof, not a direction, is what a case needs.

Simone’s reading

The pattern was there all along.

Old scars, malnutrition and a haircut just before he died point to sustained, private abuse by someone who kept him hidden. A stranger doesn’t fit the physical record.

Police say they have suspicions. No suspect has been publicly named.

Sixty-five years without a name

1953 to 2026
  1. 1953JANUARY 13Joseph Augustus Zarelli is born. His identity will not be known for almost seventy years.
  2. 1957FEBRUARY 25Frederick Benonis reports finding a boy’s body in Fox Chase, in a cardboard box that once held a J.C. Penney bassinet.
  3. 1957JULYHomicide detectives serve as pallbearers at the funeral. He is buried in a potter’s field near Dunks Ferry.
  4. 1960THE PSYCHICRemington Bristow, an investigator with the Medical Examiner’s office, follows a psychic tip to a foster home about 1.5 miles from the discovery site. He works the case until his death in 1993.
  5. 1998REBURIEDPolice re-interview the foster family and conclude they were not involved. A tooth yields too little DNA, and the boy is reburied at Ivy Hill Cemetery, still unnamed.
  6. 2019SECOND EXHUMATIONA fuller DNA sample goes to forensic genealogist Colleen Fitzpatrick.
  7. 2022DECEMBER 8Police confirm his identity. Capt. Jason Smith says they have suspicions about who is responsible.
  8. 2023–26THE LEGACYA new headstone is dedicated in 2023. Eight more unidentified remains are exhumed in 2024, and in 2026 the Vidocq Society launches a nationwide initiative for unidentified children.

No winner

  • “People refuse polygraphs for all kinds of reasons that have nothing to do with guilt.”

  • “It tells you it wasn’t one bad night.”
  • “I’m not arguing the Nicolettis did it.”
  • “We got his name back. I’m not sure we get anything else.”

← All case filesCase No. 006 · 1923 · Status: Disputed

July 20, 1923 · Parral, Chihuahua, Mexico

Pancho Villa: The Parral Ambush

Historical debate This case names real historical figures, including Álvaro Obregón and Plutarco Elías Calles, in a century-old conspiracy theory that historians still dispute. It is presented as an open question, not a settled accusation.

In July 1923 Pancho Villa was shot dead in an ambush in Parral, Chihuahua. A man confessed, was sentenced to twenty years, and walked free soon after. Who really sponsored it is still disputed. This is what Eli and Simone argued over.

Retirement, by agreement

Canutillo

Interim president Adolfo de la Huerta negotiated Villa’s amnesty, not Obregón. Villa took it and settled into farming.

  • The landA 25,000-acre hacienda at Canutillo, Durango
  • The dealA government pension
  • The guard50 of his own former fighters, government-sanctioned
  • What he builtA school and houses for the local community
  1. 01 / 04 · THE CONFESSION

    Jesús Salas Barraza, a Chihuahua state legislator, publicly confessed. He cited a grudge: Villa had once pistol-whipped him in a dispute over a woman.

  2. 02 / 04 · THE SENTENCE

    Twenty years, commuted by the governor of Chihuahua. Sources converge on a release after roughly one to three months.

  3. 03 / 04 · THE SUSPECTS

    Gen. Joaquín Amaro, who had fought Villista guerrillas, and Melitón Lozoya, the hacienda’s former owner, in a debt dispute with Villa.

  4. 04 / 04 · THE GAP

    Nobody can produce a signed order. Historians read the same facts as cover from above or as personal grudges.

Two readings of a killing

Same case
Eli’s reading

Cover from above.

A public confession followed by quiet mercy is the pattern of a man who did a job somebody wanted done. Obregón’s circle benefited and protected the confessed gunman afterward. No signed order exists, but the chain from benefit to cover does.

Simone’s reading

Three grudges, one morning.

Institutions don’t need to order what several people already wanted badly enough to plan themselves. Salas Barraza, Amaro and Lozoya each had a documented reason. The cover came after because it was convenient, not before because it was ordered.

No source settles who sponsored the ambush.

From an army to an empty road

1914 to 1926
  1. 1914THE FILM DEALThe Mutual Film Company signs a contract for exclusive rights to film Villa’s troops in battle, with Villa getting 20% of the revenue.
  2. 1916MARCH 9Villa’s forces raid Columbus, New Mexico. Gen. John J. Pershing’s Punitive Expedition of about 10,000 troops pursues him until February 1917 and never captures him.
  3. 1920CARRANZA FALLSÁlvaro Obregón, who beat Villa at Celaya in 1915, leads a revolt. Carranza flees Mexico City and is killed in May.
  4. 1920THE AMNESTYInterim president Adolfo de la Huerta gives Villa the Canutillo hacienda, a pension and a guard of 50 of his own former fighters.
  5. 1923JULY 20In Parral, a street vendor’s shout of “Viva Villa” signals gunmen. Villa is struck an estimated nine times and dies at or near instantly. Four of the five men with him are killed.
  6. 1923THE CONFESSIONJesús Salas Barraza publicly confesses and is sentenced to twenty years, but the governor commutes it and he serves only a fraction.
  7. 1923FIVE MONTHS LATERDe la Huerta, who had given Villa his amnesty, rebels openly against the Obregón and Calles government.
  8. 1926FEBRUARYVilla’s grave is broken into and his skull removed. An American, Emil Holmdahl, is implicated at the time. The skull is never recovered.

Two readings

No winner
Precision · Eli Marchetti

“I can’t hand you a signed order. Nobody can.”
